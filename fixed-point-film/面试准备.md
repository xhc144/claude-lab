## 1. “你最喜欢什么定理？”：1 分钟版本

> **English (约 80 秒)**
>
> My favorite theorem is **Brouwer's fixed point theorem**. It says that if you take a closed disk, or a solid ball in any dimension, and map it **continuously** into itself, then at least one point stays where it is. In symbols, there is a point *x* with *f(x) = x*.
>
> I like it because you can see it in daily life. If you stir a cup of coffee smoothly, without splashing, at least one point on the surface ends up exactly where it started. Or if you drop a map of a park somewhere inside that park, some point on the map lies exactly on top of the real place it shows.
>
> What I find most beautiful is the proof using **Sperner's lemma**. You cut a triangle into small triangles and color the vertices with three colors, following one simple rule. Then a **parity argument**, which is basically counting doors between rooms, shows there must be a small triangle with all three colors. If you color each vertex by the direction the map moves it, these rainbow triangles shrink down to the fixed point.
>
> In one dimension, the theorem is just the intermediate value theorem. In economics, it's the key tool behind **Nash equilibrium**.

> **中文对照**
>
> 我最喜欢的定理是布劳威尔不动点定理：把一个闭圆盘（或任意维数的闭球）连续地映到它自身，至少有一个点不动，即存在 x 使 f(x)=x。
> 它在生活里随处可见：平稳地搅咖啡（不溅出来），液面上至少有一点回到原位；把公园地图放在公园里，地图上总有一点正好压在它所表示的位置上。
> 我最喜欢的是用斯佩纳引理的证明：把三角形剖分成小三角形，按规则给顶点染三种颜色，用“数门”的奇偶性论证可知一定有三色小三角形；按映射移动的方向染色，这些三色三角形会收缩到不动点。
> 一维情形就是介值定理；在经济学里，它是纳什均衡存在性的关键工具。

**练习方法**：先跟着英文版视频做跟读（shadowing），再脱稿讲；最后练习**边画图边讲**（见第 3 节）。

## 2. “你最近在想什么数学问题？”

**推荐回答：知道存在 vs. 真正找到（existence vs. construction）**

这个话题能自然接上你的定理，体现你在思考，而且有真实的开放问题可以聊。

> **English (约 90 秒)**
>
> Recently I've been thinking about the difference between **knowing that something exists** and **actually finding it**.
>
> Brouwer's theorem tells you a fixed point exists, but not where it is. For some maps it's easy. If the map is a **contraction**, meaning it brings every two points closer together, you can just apply it again and again, and you converge to the fixed point. That's **Banach's fixed point theorem**. But for a map like rotating a disk, the fixed point is the center, yet repeating the rotation just makes points go around in circles. Iteration doesn't find it.
>
> Interestingly, Sperner's proof is actually an **algorithm**: start at a door on the boundary and keep walking through doors until you reach a rainbow triangle. But the walk can be very long. Computer scientists study this with a complexity class called **PPAD**, introduced by Christos Papadimitriou in 1994. In 2006, **Chen Xi and Deng Xiaotie** showed that even finding a Nash equilibrium of a two-player game is PPAD-complete.
>
> So the question I keep thinking about is: why can't the door-following walk be shortcut? Is finding a fixed point really as hard as it seems?

> **中文对照**：最近我在想“知道存在”和“真正找到”的区别。布劳威尔只保证存在。若映射是压缩映射，反复迭代就收敛到不动点（巴拿赫不动点定理）；但对圆盘旋转，不动点是圆心，迭代却只会绕圈。斯佩纳的证明其实是一个算法（沿着门一直走），但路径可能很长；计算机科学用 PPAD 类研究这个问题（Papadimitriou，1994），2006 年陈汐和邓小铁证明了连两人博弈的纳什均衡都是 PPAD 完全的。我一直在想：这种“沿门走”的过程为什么不能抄近路？

**面试前请真的做一下这个小实验**（5 分钟），这样你说 "I tried" 的时候是真话：

```python
import math

def contraction(x, y):   # rotate by 40 degrees, shrink to 0.6, then shift: a contraction
    c, s = 0.6 * math.cos(0.7), 0.6 * math.sin(0.7)
    return c * x - s * y + 0.3, s * x + c * y - 0.1

def rotation(x, y):      # rotate the disk by 40 degrees about its center: fixed point = (0, 0)
    c, s = math.cos(0.7), math.sin(0.7)
    return c * x - s * y, s * x + c * y

for f in (contraction, rotation):
    p = (0.5, 0.0)
    for _ in range(30):
        p = f(*p)
    print(f.__name__, p, "moved by", math.dist(p, f(*p)))
# contraction converges (moved by ~0); rotation keeps circling (moved by ~0.34)
```

做完可以加一句："I tried this on a computer: iteration converged for the contraction but just circled for the rotation."

**备选回答（换一个题目时用）：内接正方形问题（Toeplitz，1911）**
"Does every closed curve in the plane contain four points that form a square?" 对光滑曲线、凸曲线等已经证明，但对一般的连续闭曲线至今仍是开放问题。如果把“正方形”换成“长方形”，Vaughan 用默比乌斯带给出过一个非常漂亮的证明。题目简单、性质拓扑，和不动点定理气质相近。只有真的读过这个问题，才用这个备选。

## 3. 白板 / 纸上讲证明（2 分钟脚本）

先画一个大三角形，顶点标 R、G、B，再画 4×4 左右的剖分。

1. **Coloring rule.** "A vertex on a side may only use the colors of that side's two corners. Inside, any color."
2. **Rooms and doors.** "Each small triangle is a room; each red–green edge is a door."
3. **Bottom side.** "Along the bottom the color starts red and ends green, so it switches an odd number of times: an odd number of doors. The other sides have no red–green edges."
4. **Walk.** "A room has at most two doors. Enter from a bottom door and always leave through the other door. You can't loop, so you either leave through another bottom door or stop in a room with exactly one door, which is a rainbow room."
5. **Parity.** "Bottom doors pair up, but there are an odd number of them, so at least one walk ends in a rainbow room."
6. **To Brouwer.** "Use barycentric coordinates (λ_R, λ_G, λ_B), which add up to 1. Color a vertex v by a coordinate that f decreases. On a side one coordinate is 0 and cannot decrease, so this is a Sperner coloring. Refine the grid: rainbow triangles shrink to a point x\*. By continuity f(x\*)_i ≤ x\*_i for every i, and both sides sum to 1, so f(x\*) = x\*."

中文要点：边上只能用两端颜色 → 底边门数为奇数 → 每个房间最多两扇门，沿门走不会回头 → 底边的门两两配对，多出的一扇必然通向三色房间 → 按“哪个重心坐标减少”染色就是斯佩纳染色 → 网格加细，三色三角形收缩到 x\*，三个坐标都不增且和为 1，所以都不变。

**为什么三色三角形是奇数个**（老师可能追问）："Count door-sides room by room. An interior door is counted twice, a boundary door once, so the total is odd. Rainbow rooms have exactly one door, and every other room has 0 or 2. So the number of rainbow rooms is odd."

## 4. 可能的追问与参考回答

**Q1. State the theorem precisely.**
"Every continuous map from a nonempty compact convex subset of ℝⁿ to itself has a fixed point. In particular this holds for the closed unit ball. And because it's topological, it also holds for any space homeomorphic to a ball, like a square or a filled triangle."
（紧 = 有界且闭；凸 = 任两点连线在集合内。）

**Q2. Why does each condition matter?**
- Not continuous: on [0,1], jump from 0.8 to 0.2 at x = 1/2; the graph skips over y = x.
- Not closed: f(x) = x/2 on (0,1) slides toward 0, which is missing.
- Not bounded: f(x) = x + 1 on ℝ.
- A hole: rotating an annulus moves every point.

**Q3. Prove the one-dimensional case.**
"Let g(x) = f(x) − x on [0,1]. Since f maps into [0,1], g(0) = f(0) ≥ 0 and g(1) = f(1) − 1 ≤ 0. g is continuous, so by the intermediate value theorem g(c) = 0 for some c, which means f(c) = c."

**Q4. Is the fixed point unique?**
"No. The identity map fixes every point. Uniqueness needs extra conditions, for example a contraction (Banach's fixed point theorem), which also gives an algorithm: just iterate."

**Q5. How would you actually find the fixed point?**
"For contractions, iterate. In general, follow Sperner's door path on finer and finer grids; this idea is behind Herbert Scarf's algorithm from 1967. But in the worst case it can take exponentially many steps. Hirsch, Papadimitriou and Vavasis proved an exponential lower bound in the black-box model, and the problem is PPAD-complete."

**Q6. Does it hold for a sphere?**
"No. The surface of a sphere isn't a ball. The antipodal map x ↦ −x has no fixed point."

**Q7. Does it hold in infinite dimensions?**
"Not for the closed unit ball of an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. Kakutani gave a counterexample, because that ball isn't compact. Schauder's fixed point theorem restores it for compact convex sets."
（这条属于加分题，记得住再说。）

**Q8. Is there another proof?**
"Yes, the no-retraction argument. If f had no fixed point, draw the ray from f(x) through x until it hits the boundary circle. That gives a continuous map from the disk onto its boundary that fixes the boundary, a retraction. But a retraction can't exist: the circle has a hole that the disk doesn't. You can make that precise with winding numbers or the fundamental group."

**Q9. What's Nash equilibrium? How does Brouwer come in?**
"In a game, each player chooses a mixed strategy, meaning probabilities over moves. An equilibrium is a choice where nobody can do better by changing alone. In rock–paper–scissors it's (1/3, 1/3, 1/3). Nash built a continuous map on the set of strategy profiles that moves each player toward better replies. That set is a product of simplices, so it's compact and convex, and a fixed point of the map is exactly an equilibrium. His 1950 paper used Kakutani's fixed point theorem and his 1951 paper used Brouwer's. He shared the 1994 Nobel Prize in Economics."

**Q10. What's the connection with Hex?**
"In Hex, red tries to connect top and bottom, blue left and right. When the board is full, exactly one player has won; there are no draws. David Gale showed in 1979 that this fact is equivalent to Brouwer's theorem in two dimensions. The proof is again a path-following argument, like the doors in Sperner's lemma."
Extra fact: Nash also reinvented Hex in 1948 (Piet Hein invented it in 1942), and proved by strategy stealing that the first player can always win. That proof is also non-constructive: nobody knows the winning strategy for large boards.

**Q11. Who was Brouwer?**
"L. E. J. Brouwer (1881–1966), a Dutch mathematician. He proved the theorem around 1910 to 1912. Ironically, he later became the leading champion of intuitionism, which rejects non-constructive existence proofs like his own fixed point theorem."

**Q12. Where did you learn this?**
诚实回答你真实的学习来源。可以真的去读的资料：
- Francis Su, *Rental Harmony: Sperner's Lemma in Fair Division*, American Mathematical Monthly, 1999：高中生可读，讲分房租。
- David Gale, *The Game of Hex and the Brouwer Fixed-Point Theorem*, American Mathematical Monthly, 1979。
- 任一本点集拓扑或代数拓扑教材中“不动点定理 / 收缩”的章节（例如 Munkres《拓扑学》讲基本群的部分）。
